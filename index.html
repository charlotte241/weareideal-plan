The £6,000 question

£3,000 each a month by 31 December. Below is what you keep on each thing you sell, and the three sums that decide whether £6,000 is reachable. The numbers in the sums are yours to put down, not ours.

Where you are today

SPE nets about £1,754 a month, from your half-year accounts to June. Lettings profit sits inside the lease. Development is negative until the current scheme completes.

£6,000 a month of new profit

That is the gap. Everything below is a way of closing part of it.

Not what you charge. What is left after the room costs money to put on. Your direct SPE costs are £1,100 a month and about 61 people are in the room, so every head in that room costs roughly £18.

Ticket price is every paid ticket across January to March 2026 from your attendance sheet. Cost per head is your £6,597 of direct SPE costs for the half year, divided by six months and by the 61 people in the room. A member is £144 for six meetings. Zoom carries no venue or catering cost, so nearly all of it is kept. Sponsorship is paid whether or not anyone turns up, so it carries no extra cost per head.

The room cannot produce £6,000. At £12.85 a ticket you would need 467 more paid tickets every month. The room holds sixty. Seven more paid tickets and twelve more members, which would be a good year, comes to about £160 a month. Tickets and membership are worth doing because they hold the room together. They are not where the money is.

So where does £6,000 come from?

Three places, and only two of them are inside your control before Christmas. Work these out in this order.

Sum one · what one more property is worth
  • Management income is about £4,500 a month. Divide it by your number of rooms. That is income per room.
  • Now the same for team cost. Mithila says the two are about level, so start there.
  • The question is not what a room earns. It is whether the current team can take on more rooms without growing. If they can, a new room is worth close to the full fee. If they cannot, it is worth almost nothing.
  • Write down one number: what one more managed property adds to profit each month.
Sum two · what sponsorship is worth
  • Fourteen sponsors pay about £1,019 a month between them. Five main, nine marketplace.
  • Go through them one at a time and write down what each one gets today.
  • Then ask the other way round: if you were buying a year in front of forty property people, what would you pay?
  • Write down one number: what the fourteen should be paying, and what the unsold sixth category is worth.
Sum three · the lease
  • Nearly all current profit sits in it, and you have parked the conversation until after Christmas.
  • If the first two sums do not reach £6,000, this is the only lever left that is big enough.
  • Write down one number: what moving the lease would be worth, and whether after Christmas is still the right time.
Three numbers and you have a plan. Property, sponsorship, lease. Everything else on this page is worth a few hundred pounds a month and can be decided later. Forty five minutes, the two of you, in that order.
